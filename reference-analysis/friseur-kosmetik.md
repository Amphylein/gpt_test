# Reference Analysis — Friseur / Kosmetikstudio

Research snapshot: **2026-08-25**

## What a strong salon/beauty website must solve

Beauty websites are unusually sensitive to generic aesthetics. A weak site quickly looks like a purchased template because the category is full of beige palettes, serif headlines, pink gradients and stock model photography.

A strong site has to make four things feel credible:

- the atmosphere
- the quality/taste level
- the people doing the work
- the booking path

Unlike Handwerk, the service result is personal. The team and visual taste are part of the product.

## Reference 1 — Jo Hansford

Live site: https://johansford.com/

### Why it matters

Jo Hansford is a useful premium reference because it behaves like a mature luxury beauty brand rather than a local salon template.

### Strong decisions

- one powerful category position: London's leading hair destination
- services split cleanly into colour, styling and beauty
- editorial photography is integrated with service hierarchy
- press logos and the Royal Warrant are genuine, high-value proof
- online booking is easy to reach
- commerce/product content exists without taking over the salon experience
- local contact details and opening hours are obvious
- Instagram is framed as an extension of the brand world rather than a random feed

### What to learn

Premium trust should come from **real authority and visual consistency**, not from gold buttons and fake “luxury” copy.

### What not to copy

This brand can credibly use institutional press/royal proof that a new local salon cannot. Our demo must never fabricate equivalent badges.

## Reference 2 — Salon Lavender

Live site: https://www.salonlavender.com/

### Why it matters

Lavender has a distinctive point of view: holistic hair, botanical beauty and sustainability. The website is useful because the visual and verbal identity grows directly from the business philosophy.

### Strong decisions

- clear niche statement in the hero
- wellness/sustainability is expressed as a real operating philosophy
- service descriptions have personality rather than only prices
- team has a dedicated place in the homepage story
- booking is persistent and easy
- imagery supports organic/tactile positioning
- sustainability is a full section, not a tiny green badge

### What to learn

The easiest way to avoid “generic salon AI website” is to define a **specific worldview** for the salon.

### What not to copy

Do not force sustainability/botanical language onto a salon that is really about fashion colour, barbering or clinical cosmetics. The point is coherence, not the aesthetic itself.

## Reference 3 — Eight Five Zero Salon

Live site: https://eightfivezerosalon.com/

### Why it matters

Eight Five Zero is structurally useful for a more mainstream premium salon. It shows how offers, services, products, team, booking and reviews can coexist without requiring an avant-garde interface.

### Strong decisions

- appointment booking sits in the primary navigation
- new-guest offer is a dedicated conversion path
- service taxonomy is understandable
- team/reviews are easy to find
- gift cards and products provide secondary revenue paths
- short, energetic hero copy fits the category

### What to learn

A salon website can be commercially effective without becoming visually complicated.

### What not to copy

Image sliders and motivational micro-headlines can easily become dated or template-like. Our demo should use fewer, stronger hero moments.

---

## Reference 4 — dbk Salon

Live site: https://dbksalon.com/

### Why it matters

The main dbk site is unusually simple: it acts primarily as a location selector for several salons, each with its own booking/website path.

### Strong decisions

- immediate brand line: “Confident. Unique. Beautiful.”
- locations are the main decision, not buried in a footer
- every location offers booking directly
- phone/address are visible at the selection point
- the site does not overcomplicate a routing problem

### What to learn

When a business has multiple locations, the information architecture should change. Do not build one huge generic page and hide location differences.

### What not to copy

The hub is intentionally sparse. A single-location salon demo needs far more atmosphere, service proof and team content.

# Cross-reference conclusions

## Pattern 1 — The brand needs a point of view

Strong beauty sites can be:

- fashion/editorial
- botanical/wellness
- technical colour authority
- playful/young
- clinical/skin-science
- minimalist premium

But they should not be all of them at once.

## Pattern 2 — Team is product

For many clients, choosing the stylist/beautician matters almost as much as choosing the service.

The demo should therefore support:

- team portrait
- specialties
- experience/approach
- optional starting-price tier
- direct booking with that person where the booking platform supports it

Avoid fake biographies in production.

## Pattern 3 — Price transparency reduces anxiety

A demo should show either:

- exact prices
- “from” prices
- consultation requirement for variable services

Hiding every price behind “contact us” is poor UX for mainstream salons.

## Pattern 4 — Booking is the main CTA

Primary CTA should almost always be:

`Termin buchen`

or, for consultation-driven cosmetics:

`Beratung buchen`

Do not make visitors hunt for the booking system.

## Pattern 5 — Real work should replace generic model photography

A real client version should prioritize:

- actual salon interior
- actual stylists
- actual results
- real texture/detail shots

For the demo, properly licensed editorial hair/beauty imagery is acceptable, but it should never falsely imply that a model is a real employee.

## Recommended synthesis for our demo

Build a **modern single-location salon / beauty studio** with a clear concept, e.g.:

- contemporary colour & cut studio
- botanical salon
- skin/beauty studio with calm clinical edge

For the first demo, a contemporary hair + beauty studio gives enough visual range without needing medical-style claims.

Borrow:

- authority and service clarity from Jo Hansford
- coherent philosophy from Lavender
- commercial booking logic from Eight Five Zero
- location simplicity from dbk where relevant

## Suggested homepage story

1. compact nav + persistent `Termin buchen`
2. editorial hero, one image or restrained split composition
3. short brand statement with actual point of view
4. 3–4 core service categories
5. large result/atmosphere image
6. team preview with specialties
7. pricing/consultation logic
8. salon atmosphere / philosophy
9. reviews
10. location + opening hours
11. booking CTA
12. footer with social/contact

## Visual direction

- no pink/purple gradient by default
- no gold-on-black pseudo-luxury cliché
- no 3-column icon feature grid
- no fake cosmetics packaging
- use typography like a fashion/editorial system, but keep service information highly readable
- choose either soft/organic OR sharp/editorial; do not blend five styles
- use subtle motion on imagery only
- keep booking controls obvious and conventional
